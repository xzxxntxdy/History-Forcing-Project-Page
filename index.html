Remember what came before. Generate what comes next.

Abstract

Few-step autoregressive video diffusion enables efficient generation, yet maintaining consistency over long rollouts remains challenging, often manifesting in subject and scene drift and inconsistent continuations after out-of-sight intervals. Despite progress in fidelity and rollout robustness, existing distillation methods do not fully exploit richer historical information to constrain the student's current prediction. To address this gap, we introduce History Forcing, a unified on-policy distillation framework that strengthens historical supervision for few-step autoregressive video generators. The framework uses the difference between teacher predictions with and without informative historical context to decouple this supervision. Such context can be obtained from earlier generated segments with less accumulated drift or from a fixed-view teacher rollout that keeps the scene visible while the student looks away. By aligning the student with this history-conditioned target through distillation, History Forcing incorporates long-range historical constraints into autoregressive generation. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on VBench and VBench-Long among 1.3B models for 15s and 60s video generation, respectively. Evaluations on PlayWorld and MemoBench further show improved out-of-sight world modeling, with more plausible scene evolution and more consistent states when the camera returns.

04 Method

Distill the difference history makes.

A unified training principle connects long-horizon consistency with out-of-sight evolution.

01 / ROLLOUT

Learn on the student’s own history.

Generate on-policy autoregressive rollouts, exposing training to the contexts the student encounters at inference.

02 / CONTRAST

Ask what the past changes.

Hold the noisy chunk, timestep, and recent context fixed. Compare teacher predictions with and without additional informative history.

03 / DISTILL

Guide the next continuation.

Transfer this history-dependent difference to the DMD reference score or the EMA teacher’s velocity target.

One principle, two applications

Preserve quality.
Strengthen historical constraints.

For long videos, history comes from earlier student-generated segments. For world models, it comes from a fixed-view teacher that keeps watching as the student looks away.
